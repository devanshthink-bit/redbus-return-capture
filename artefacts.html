RedBus — Return capture at checkout

Every artefact from the provisional bet through to the design brief, in the order it was made. 8 interviews · 102 atomic notes · 6 clusters · survey n=23 qualified from 61.

01 · Scope Card

The bet, placed before any user contact.

Industry + Product
Travel & Mobility — RedBus, Type B: Return-capture at checkout
Target user
24-yo working professional in Bengaluru, ₹40–60K/mo; frequently takes intercity round trips by bus, typically for family visits, vacations, festivals or short stays, and books bus tickets online — books the outbound right away but leaves the return for later
AARRR stage
Revenue (primary)
Feature / flow
Return booking during the outbound booking journey (Search → Bus Listing → Seat Selection → Review → Payment)
Provisional metric
In-session return-attach rate: % of one-way booking sessions converted into round-trip bookings
Provisional hypothesis
I hypothesise that travellers defer the return because fixing a return date/seat feels risky when plans aren't firm, rather than lack of intent or price. Lowering commitment risk lifts in-session return-attach rate — to test in research
Scope sentence

“I’m designing the return-capture decision at checkout for young professionals who frequently take intercity round trips by bus, targeting Revenue, to lift in-session return-attach rate, because I currently believe travellers postpone booking their return due to commitment risk rather than lack of intent.”

02 · Research Plan

What was planned, and what actually came back.

Research question
Why do intercity round-trip bus travellers often book the outbound journey immediately but postpone booking the return leg on RedBus?
What I assume now
Travellers defer the return because fixing a return date/seat feels risky when plans aren't firm, rather than lack of intent or price. Lowering commitment risk lifts in-session return-attach rate — to test in research
Primary method + why
1:1 user interviews — it's a why question about behaviour and motivation
Secondary / triangulation
Micro-survey + App Store reviews + Reddit
Who I'll talk to
22–30-yo metro-based migrant professionals who booked an online intercity bus round trip in the last six months and booked the outbound and return separately (or abandoned the return), plus 1–2 who booked both legs together, for comparison
“Enough” signal
The last 3 of ~5–6 interviews surface the same patterns, the same 2–3 reasons keep repeating — enough to keep, refine or kill the hypothesis

04 · Clusters

Six clusters. Each carries the tension underneath it and a So what? written from the traveller’s side.

C1

User can't commit to a return date that isn't theirs to set

Tension

The date they'd have to commit to is owned by someone or something else

What they did

Flew out with no return booked and waited until an event, a group or a recovery resolved itself — Jaimin, Sai, Prayani, Vivek, Soumya, Samarth

So what? Even when a traveller fully intends to return, they cannot name a date that belongs to an event, a group or a recovery — so the only honest answer at checkout is to leave without a return.
n04 n10 n16 n24 n34 n42 n43 n50 n59 n67 n68
C2

User avoids booking at all, because every way of staying flexible costs money

Tension

Undoing a commitment costs real money, so they avoid taking the position at all

What they did

Skipped the cancellation add-on and simply didn't book, rather than book and risk the 20/40/80% cancellation ladder — Vivek, Samarth, Anand, Soumya, Harshraj

So what? Every way of keeping the right to change costs money, up front or on exit — so travellers protect themselves by never taking the position at all. Not booking is the cheapest insurance available to them.
n07 n12 n45 n49 n56 n57 n62 n64 n70 n76
C3

User only finds out what waiting cost them after the decision is made

Tension

Deferring feels free at the moment of choosing and expensive a week later

What they did

Came back to an emptier bus — seat gone, price up, group split across two buses — Soumya, Vivek, Sai

So what? Waiting looks free at the moment of choosing and only shows its price a week later — so travellers keep making a choice they would not make if the cost were visible while they were making it.
n33 n51 n52 n60 n61 n71 n74
C4

User has to start the return search from scratch, because nothing is carried forward

Tension

The return is a fresh search later, not a thing already in progress

What they did

Re-found the return from scratch days later, sometimes on a different app — and one ₹50 pop-up was enough to convert one of them — Harshraj, Jaimin, Sai, Vivek

So what? The work already done — route, operator, seat, boarding point — is held nowhere, so coming back to book means starting the same job from zero. Some of them finish it on another app.
n11 n17 n35 n65 n66 n73 n75 n78
C5

User carries the unbooked return around for the whole trip

Tension

An open return is not free — it occupies them until it's closed

What they did

Described booking both as relief, and described the open return as something they kept thinking about mid-trip — Sai, Prayani, Jaimin, Soumya

So what? An open return is not free. It follows the traveller through the trip as an unfinished task, and the only way to close it is to commit to a date they still do not have.
n18 n19 n29 n30 n39 n40 n53 n58
C6THIN

User is guessing which way the fare will move, and acts on the guess

Tension

They're making a timing bet with no information, and their beliefs directly contradict each other

What they did

One waited deliberately and halved his fare; two others stopped waiting after concluding prices only rise; one refuses to re-check at all in case checking raises the price — Anand, Sai, Soumya, Samarth

So what? Travellers are making a timing bet with no information, and they reach directly opposite conclusions from the same experience — so whichever way they act, they are guessing with their own money.

07 · Insight Statements

[User segment] struggles with [problem] because [root cause], which means [implication for design]. One per final JTBD. Root cause and implication for design are the designer’s interpretation — the segment and the problem come straight from the notes.

J1

Holding a return without owning the date

from C1 + C5
JTBD When my return depends on something that hasn't resolved yet, I want the return handled without naming a date I don't have, so I can get on with the trip instead of carrying it around with me.

Intercity round-trip travellers whose return depends on an event, a group or a recovery that hasn't resolved struggles with leaving on the outbound with no return at all, and then carrying that unbooked return through the whole trip because RedBus has only two states for a return — bought, or nothing — and both require a date the traveller doesn't own. There is nothing to commit to that matches what they actually know about their trip, which means the product needs a third state: a return they can hold using what they do know — their constraint — rather than what they don't.

J2

Being wrong cheaply

from C2
JTBD When I might be wrong about my return, I want being wrong to cost me very little, so I can decide now instead of waiting.

Travellers who think their return plans might still change struggles with choosing not to book at all, rather than book and risk what it costs to get out because the price of being wrong is set by a 20/40/80% cancellation ladder and a paid add-on, and both are priced and explained after the decision point — so the cheapest way to stay flexible is to take no position at all, which means the cost of being wrong has to be knowable and small at the moment of committing, not discoverable afterwards.

J3

Deciding to wait with the price of waiting visible

from C3 + C6
JTBD When I'm deciding whether to book the return now or later, I want to know what waiting will cost me, so I'm not making a money decision on a guess.

Travellers deciding whether to book the return now or later struggles with deferring on a hunch, then discovering the price a week later — a lost seat, a higher fare, a group split across two buses because RedBus holds the seat and fare history for every route and shows none of it at the moment the traveller is choosing to wait, so a decision with real money on it is made blind — and travellers reach directly opposite conclusions from the same experience, which means the cost of waiting has to be visible while they are deciding to wait, and stated as information rather than as urgency.

08 · Problem Statement

What survived contact with the research.

A traveller who doesn’t yet know their return date has no usable way to keep a return on RedBus.

The flexibility already exists — eligible buses allow a free date change, and a cancellation add-on is sold at checkout. But it is offered as a property of a bus, discovered incidentally, and it never enters the moment the return is actually decided. Nothing in the flow asks what the traveller does know about their trip; it asks only for a date.

So they leave the session with nothing and repeat the entire search days later, usually for a worse seat at a higher fare.

The barrier is not intent, not awareness, and not a missing feature. It is that the product’s flexibility is shaped as an attribute rather than as a way to book.

Short form

RedBus already sells the flexibility a return decision needs. It never shows up in the return decision.

09 · The Business Lens

Worked out before the solution, not after. How RedBus makes money, the number that proves value, the chain from this design decision to that number, and which loop it belongs to.

Business model → strategy → design pressure
Business model

Transaction marketplace

A commission on every ticket, paid by the operator — reported at roughly 10–20%, and the large majority of revenue. A second line, the seat-inventory software sold to operators, is untouched by this work. Owned by MakeMyTrip. Figures are publicly reported, not verified here.

→
Strategic bet, as it bears on this work

Win the whole journey, not half of it

A round trip closed in one session is two commissions at one acquisition cost. The second leg is already intended by the traveller — 73.9% book it eventually, but 26.1% book it somewhere else.

→
Design pressure this creates

Frequency and trust

A transaction model rewards fast, frictionless and confidence-building. It punishes anything that slows the outbound — which is why the return step is skippable, never modal, and sits behind a guardrail rather than a target.
